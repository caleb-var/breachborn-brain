Spatial hierarchy qualification direction

Hypothesis, not final selection

Use one coordinate and spatial identity convention, not a universal physical grid. Test a global coarse macro image and spatial index, sparse or compact regional features/pages, deterministic fine terrain, and domain-specific solvers. Start with 4 km macro candidates for the 4,000 km world and compare 1 km and 250 m land/refinement levels. At 256 km, compare the same physical scales and a denser macro source where worthwhile. Do not prescribe all-domain level sizes until actual kernel qualification.

Memory sizing

Counts use ceiling division for complete square coverage. Excludes vertical layers, graph links, caches, edits, topology, scratch, snapshots and saves.

A dense 250 m field set across 4,000 km would exceed 15 GiB before any other systems. Dense 1 km costs about 977 MiB even at only 64 B/cell. A 4 km global field is about 61 MiB. This is the reason to avoid hard-coding one resolution for every process. It does not forbid compact, compressed or sparse finer fields with proven budgets.

A 16 km regional page at 250 m contains 4096 cells, costing 256 KiB at 64 B/cell. 1024 such pages cost 256 MiB for that field set, covering 262,144 km², not the whole 4,000 km world. Pages with permanent edits or active storage must remain durably represented even when not resident. Use these values as workload probes, not fixed allocation targets.

Coarse geometry is not exact fine solid truth; refinement must agree with explicit drainage and passage constraints. A coarse water store can represent many fine locations, but distinct sealed basins cannot share one undifferentiated water level. Use partitions or connectivity-aware features. An aggregate is not a second owner: promote by partitioning amounts and restrict by summing, with appropriate clocks and deterministic/validated reconstruction.

Clock and work scaling

A full linear pass over 16 million 1 km cells every simulated second costs 0.4 CPU seconds/game-second even at an optimistic 25 ns/cell, already 16x the historical 25 ms environment target. For one million 4 km cells the same hypothetical pass costs 25 ms. These are arithmetic examples, not measured performance. Use process-specific intervals, event/due work, compact field layouts and a complete inclusive budget. A severe island-wide storm must not force a fine-cell update everywhere.

First experiment

Use the qualification plan. Compare candidate costs at both world sizes and demonstrate preserved narrow channels and causal offscreen flooding. Final selection requires a measured connected implementation; no runtime launch is authorized by this document.

Source-screened selection update (25 November 2026)

Spatial architecture selection selects a simple global macro field + 16 km regional directory + sparse feature geometry + existing Zylann fine voxel residency for the pilot. The 1 km (256 km world) and approximately 4 km (4,000 km world) macro levels and 250 m regional resolution are comparison settings, not globally imposed. Prioritize the MIT Procedural World Generation donor for CPU channel/valley realization. Regional tributary topology and closed-basin reconciliation are still unresolved. Do not install a second voxel tree or generic AMR runtime. This is a provisional engineering selection; source inspected, not executed or accepted.

Formation review supplement

The same spatial hierarchy can consume either candidate’s exported geography. Formation comparison reopens the macro generator selection and identifies existing deterministic creek growth in Procedural World Generation. No universal grid or second terrain authority is introduced.
